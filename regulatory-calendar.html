🏛️ IRDAI — Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority
IRDAI/Life/CIR/2024/156

AI Model Risk Management Guidelines

Effective Q1 2025
Live — Compliance Required

What's required: Insurers using AI/ML models for underwriting, claims, or pricing must document model risk policies, conduct pre-deployment validation, maintain model inventories, and submit annual model risk reports to IRDAI. Board-level sign-off on model risk governance framework required.

IRDAI/Tech/CIR/2024/203

AI Explainability Requirements for Policyholder Decisions

Effective Q2 2025
Live

What's required: AI decisions affecting policyholders — claim settlements, underwriting decisions, premium pricing — must be explainable in plain language. Model decision logs required for audit. Insurers must provide policyholders with a human-readable explanation on request within 30 days.

AI Ethics Chapter — Bias Testing & Fairness Audits

Q3 2025
Final Draft Issued

What's required: Mandatory AI ethics framework covering bias testing, algorithmic fairness audits for all AI-touched policyholder interactions. Gender, age, and geography bias assessments required annually. Insurers must document corrective actions when bias thresholds are exceeded.

Complete data lineage documentation required for all AI models in production. Third-party AI models subject to same governance standards as in-house models.

📋 DPDP — Digital Personal Data Protection Act
DPDP Act 2023

Implementation Rules — AI Systems Processing Personal Data

Rules notified 2024
Rolling enforcement from 2025

What's required: Consent management for all personal data processed by AI systems. Data fiduciary obligations including purpose limitation, storage minimisation, and accuracy requirements. Specific restrictions on processing children's data by AI. Cross-border transfer rules apply to AI training data and inference outputs. Data Principal rights (access, correction, erasure) must be honoured by AI systems.

Adversarial robustness requirements, model inversion attack protections, and supply chain security for AI components. Incident reporting to CERT-In within 6 hours for AI-related security breaches.

European Union AI Regulatory Landscape

🇪🇺 EU AI Act — Enforcement Timeline

The EU AI Act (Regulation 2024/1689) creates a risk-based framework with obligations phased in over three years. Each deadline below is legally binding for organisations operating in or serving the EU market.

Aug 2024
Effective

Act enters into force Completed

The EU AI Act officially entered into force on 1 August 2024 following publication in the Official Journal. The 24-month implementation clock started. All organisations with AI systems in scope should have begun gap assessments.

Feb 2025
High Priority

Prohibited AI practices ban effective High Priority

Chapter II prohibitions on unacceptable risk AI systems became enforceable. Includes: real-time biometric surveillance in public spaces, social scoring by public authorities, AI exploitation of vulnerabilities, subliminal manipulation. Non-compliance penalties up to €35M or 7% of global turnover.

Aug 2025
Upcoming

GPAI model rules & governance obligations effective Prepare Now

General Purpose AI model obligations activate. Providers of GPAI models must maintain technical documentation, comply with copyright obligations, publish training data summaries. GPAI models with systemic risk (>10²⁵ FLOPs) face additional red-teaming and incident reporting requirements. National competent authorities must be designated by Member States.

Aug 2026
Critical

High-risk AI system requirements effective Critical Deadline

Full obligations for high-risk AI systems in Annex III: financial services AI (credit scoring, insurance underwriting), employment AI (recruitment, performance monitoring), healthcare AI (diagnosis, treatment), education AI (assessment), and law enforcement AI. Requirements include: conformity assessments, CE marking, registration in EU database, human oversight measures, logging and audit trails, technical documentation (Article 11 compliant).

CHEQ Coverage
CHEQ maps 340+ EU AI Act obligations across Annex III categories. Automated conformity assessment checklists, technical documentation templates, and audit trail generation. BFSI-specific coverage for credit and insurance AI. Contact us for an EU AI Act gap scan.
Aug 2027
Final phase

Embedded AI in existing products must comply Plan Ahead

AI systems embedded in products already covered by EU product safety legislation (medical devices, machinery, vehicles) must comply with the AI Act. Legacy products with AI components that were placed on market before August 2026 get one additional year — but the clock is already running. Product roadmaps should account for this deadline now.

🔐 DORA — Digital Operational Resilience Act
Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 — DORA

ICT Risk Management for Financial Entities — AI/ML Systems in Scope

Fully applicable January 2025
Live — Enforcement Active

What's required: DORA applies to banks, insurers, investment firms, payment institutions, and ICT third-party providers across the EU. AI and ML systems are covered as ICT assets. Requirements include: ICT risk management framework, classification and documentation of all ICT assets (including AI models), incident reporting (major ICT incidents within 4 hours of classification), ICT third-party risk management (due diligence on AI vendors), and digital operational resilience testing (penetration testing, threat-led testing for significant institutions).

CHEQ Coverage
CHEQ DORA module covers ICT asset classification for AI, incident logging, and third-party AI vendor due diligence checklists. Integrates with QAVE for resilience testing evidence. 47 DORA obligations tracked in CHEQ's EU obligation library.

United States AI Regulatory Landscape

The US regulatory environment for AI is fragmented across federal agencies and states. Federal voluntary frameworks are increasingly being cited in contracts and procurement, while state-level legislation is moving faster than federal action. Compliance teams should monitor both tracks.

🏛️ Federal — Executive Orders & Frameworks
Executive Order 14110

Safe, Secure, and Trustworthy AI — Federal Agency Implementation

Ongoing 2025
Federal Implementation

What's required: Federal agencies are implementing AI safety and governance requirements under EO 14110. Key obligations for AI vendors doing federal business include: mandatory red-team testing for frontier AI models, safety evaluation results reporting, implementation of AI safety standards (NIST AI RMF). Agencies are embedding AI safety requirements into procurement contracts — companies bidding on federal work increasingly face contractual AI governance requirements.

CHEQ Coverage
QAVE red teaming modules directly address EO 14110 safety evaluation requirements. CHEQ compliance reports are structured to map against NIST AI RMF categories. Relevant for companies seeking federal contracts.
NIST

NIST AI RMF 1.0 — AI Risk Management Framework

Voluntary — Contractually Adopted
De Facto Standard

What's required: Voluntary but contractually required by a growing number of federal primes, healthcare organisations, and financial institutions. NIST AI RMF 1.0 defines four functions — Govern, Map, Measure, Manage — for AI risk management. NIST AI RMF 2.0 (GOVERN expansion) released in 2024 with additional guidance on agentic AI and generative AI risks.

CHEQ Coverage
CHEQ maps to all four NIST AI RMF functions. Evidence packages for GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE, and MANAGE available for enterprise customers. RMF alignment reports for procurement submissions included in CHEQ Enterprise tier.
🗺️ State AI Legislation — Key Dates
Colorado SB 205

Colorado AI Act — High-Risk AI System Obligations

Effective February 1, 2026
Plan Now

What's required: Developers and deployers of high-risk AI systems interacting with Colorado consumers must exercise reasonable care to prevent algorithmic discrimination. Impact assessments required, with disclosure obligations to affected consumers. Covers AI used in consequential decisions including employment, housing, education, healthcare, financial services, and insurance. First US state-level law closely modelled on EU AI Act risk-based approach.

California AB 2013

California AI Transparency Act — Training Data Disclosure

Effective January 1, 2026
Plan Now

What's required: AI providers that make GenAI systems publicly available in California must post on their website documentation about training data — including datasets used, data sources, categories of personal data, and any synthetic data. Applies to systems available on or after January 1, 2022. Significant implications for AI product companies operating in or serving California.

Illinois HB 3773

Illinois AI in Employment Act

Effective January 1, 2026
Employment AI

What's required: Employers using AI to make or substantially assist in employment decisions — hiring, promotion, pay, termination — must notify employees and applicants. Annual bias audits required for AI used in employment decisions. Disclosure of AI use in the application and performance review process. Covers employers with 15+ employees operating in Illinois.

UAE & Gulf AI Regulatory Landscape

The UAE and Gulf region is rapidly building AI governance infrastructure, anchored by the UAE National AI Strategy 2031. Financial services regulators in ADGM and DIFC have issued substantive AI guidance, while the broader UAE and Saudi PDPL frameworks create data governance obligations that apply directly to AI systems.

🇦🇪 UAE — National & Sector Frameworks
UAE Government

UAE National AI Strategy 2031 — Implementation Obligations

Ongoing
Active Implementation

What's required: UAE National AI Strategy 2031 sets out sector-specific AI adoption targets across government, healthcare, energy, and financial services. Government entities and strategic partners are expected to demonstrate AI governance maturity. Alignment with UAE AI ethics principles — fairness, transparency, accountability, privacy, safety — is increasingly referenced in government procurement and partnership evaluations.

ADGM Financial Services Regulatory Authority

ADGM AI Guidance for Financial Services

Effective 2024
Live

What's required: ADGM-regulated financial services firms using AI in regulated activities must demonstrate: model governance frameworks, explainability for customer-facing AI decisions, ongoing monitoring and drift detection, board-level accountability for AI outcomes. Firms are expected to align with IOSCO's AI/ML recommendations and international best practices including NIST AI RMF.

CHEQ Coverage
CHEQ ADGM module covers model governance, explainability, and drift detection requirements. QAVE provides ongoing monitoring with alerting for ADGM-regulated AI systems. Coverage available for firms operating in Abu Dhabi Global Market.
Federal Decree-Law No. 45/2021

UAE Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL) — AI Systems

Enforcement from 2024
Enforcement Active

What's required: UAE PDPL applies to all AI systems processing personal data of UAE residents. Obligations include: lawful basis for data processing, purpose limitation, data subject rights (access, correction, deletion), cross-border transfer restrictions, data breach notification (within 72 hours), and data protection impact assessments for high-risk processing. AI systems performing automated decision-making affecting data subjects must provide opt-out mechanisms.

CHEQ Coverage
CHEQ UAE PDPL module maps 28 core obligations. Automated DPIA generation, data subject rights workflow, and breach notification audit trails included. Available for CHEQ Enterprise customers.
🇸🇦 Saudi Arabia & DIFC
Saudi Arabia — SDAIA

Saudi Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL) — 2024 AI-Specific Provisions

Original 2022 · Updated 2024
Live

What's required: Saudi PDPL amendments effective 2024 include AI-specific provisions addressing automated decision-making, profiling, and sensitive data processing by AI systems. Controllers using AI for profiling must register with the National Data Management Office (NDMO). Cross-border data transfer for AI training data requires SDAIA approval. Updated implementing regulations introduce fines up to SAR 5 million for data breaches involving AI systems.

DIFC Data Protection Law 2020 — Amendment 2023

DIFC DP Law — Automated Processing & AI Obligations

Applies to AI systems from 2024
Active

What's required: DIFC Data Protection Law applies to organisations processing personal data within the Dubai International Financial Centre. 2023 amendments strengthen automated decision-making provisions directly applicable to AI. Requirements: data subject rights for automated decisions, mandatory DPIAs for high-risk AI processing, data processor agreements covering AI sub-processors, and appointment of a Data Protection Officer where processing is large-scale. GDPR-equivalent standard — organisations with EU compliance programs can typically leverage existing documentation.
