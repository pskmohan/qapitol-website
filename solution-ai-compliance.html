The Regulatory Landscape

Four frameworks. All enforceable. All applying to your AI systems right now.

Ignorance is not a defence. Each of these frameworks carries real consequences — from procurement disqualification to regulatory enforcement action. Knowing which ones apply to your AI systems is the first step to compliance. Qapitol maps them all.

🇪🇺
EU AI Act

EU Artificial Intelligence Act

The world’s first comprehensive binding legal framework for AI — and the gold standard other jurisdictions are now emulating.

  • Risk classification: prohibited, high-risk, limited-risk, and minimal-risk AI systems
  • Mandatory conformity assessments for high-risk AI before market placement
  • Prohibited AI practices: social scoring, real-time biometric surveillance in public spaces, subliminal manipulation
  • High-risk system requirements: human oversight, transparency, robustness, accuracy, and data governance obligations
  • Foundation model providers: transparency, adversarial testing, and incident reporting obligations
⏰ Effective 2024 – 2026 (phased enforcement)
🇺🇸
NIST AI RMF

NIST AI Risk Management Framework

Voluntary when launched; now appearing as a mandatory requirement in US federal procurement contracts and enterprise supplier assessments.

  • Govern — establish AI risk governance policies, accountability structures, and culture
  • Map — categorise AI systems by context, capabilities, and risk exposure
  • Measure — analyse, assess, and track AI risks using defined metrics and testing protocols
  • Manage — prioritise and respond to AI risks with documented treatment plans and controls
  • Now referenced in CISA, DOD, and federal agency procurement requirements
⏰ Written into US federal procurement (2024 onwards)
🇮🇳
RBI AI Guidelines

RBI AI & Model Risk Guidelines

India’s Reserve Bank of India has moved decisively on AI governance — with direct implications for any bank, NBFC, or fintech using AI in regulated financial processes.

  • Model Risk Management Circular: validation, documentation, and ongoing monitoring of all AI/ML models
  • India-specific guidelines for AI use in credit decisions, fraud detection, and customer-facing applications
  • Board-level AI governance requirements for systemically important financial institutions
  • SEBI guidance on algorithmic trading and AI use in broking and advisory services
  • Anti-discrimination requirements: AI must not produce biased outcomes in lending or insurance
⏰ Active — with escalating audit focus in 2025
📋
ISO/IEC 42001

ISO/IEC 42001 — AI Management System

The international standard for AI management systems — now appearing as a procurement requirement from large enterprises in regulated sectors who need supplier AI assurance.

  • AI management system standard: policies, roles, objectives, and continual improvement for AI governance
  • Risk and impact assessment requirements for AI systems throughout their lifecycle
  • Supplier and third-party AI risk management obligations
  • Now required or preferred in procurement by enterprises in financial services, healthcare, and public sector
  • Certification pathway available — Qapitol provides gap assessment and remediation support
⏰ Now required in enterprise procurement (2024+)
What Qapitol Assesses

Eight compliance domains. Every AI system evaluated against all of them.

Regulatory compliance for AI is not a single checkbox — it spans governance, data, models, operations, and oversight. Qapitol assesses across all eight domains in a structured gap register, rated Red / Amber / Green against applicable framework requirements.

🎯

Risk Classification

Determine whether your AI systems fall into prohibited, high-risk, or limited-risk categories under EU AI Act and equivalent frameworks. Most organisations get this wrong — and the consequences of misclassification are severe.

🗄️

Data Governance

Training data lineage, data quality documentation, sensitive data handling, consent frameworks, and data minimisation requirements. All EU AI Act high-risk systems must demonstrate robust data governance before deployment.

📄

Model Documentation

Technical documentation, model cards, capability and limitation statements, performance benchmarks, and version control. Regulators require documentation to exist before an audit — not to be created in response to one.

⚖️

Bias & Fairness Testing

Systematic evaluation of AI outputs for discriminatory patterns across protected characteristics. Required under EU AI Act for high-risk systems and under RBI guidelines for credit AI. Qapitol delivers structured bias audit reports with statistical evidence.

🧑‍💻

Human Oversight Mechanisms

Controls ensuring humans can monitor, override, and intervene in AI system decisions. EU AI Act mandates meaningful human oversight for all high-risk systems — "human in the loop" must be real, not nominal.

🚨

Incident Response for AI

Documented procedures for detecting, reporting, and responding to AI system failures, unintended behaviours, and serious incidents. EU AI Act includes mandatory incident reporting to national authorities for high-risk systems.

🔍

Audit Trail & Explainability

Logging infrastructure, decision traceability, and explainability mechanisms sufficient to support regulatory review and legal challenge. Explainability is not aspirational — it is a legal requirement in high-stakes AI contexts.

🤝

Third-Party AI Vendor Risk

Assessment of compliance obligations when using external AI systems, foundation models, or AI-powered SaaS. EU AI Act downstream obligations mean your vendor’s compliance posture is now your compliance risk.

Sectors Where This Is Urgent

Four sectors where AI compliance is no longer optional — and the consequences of non-compliance are immediate.

Regulatory enforcement is not uniform. These four sectors face the sharpest near-term risk — where an AI audit is not theoretical, it is scheduled.

🏦

BFSI — Banking, Financial Services & Insurance

Highest Urgency — Enforcement Active

RBI model risk circulars require documented validation and ongoing monitoring for every AI/ML model used in credit decisions, fraud detection, or customer risk assessment. SEBI has issued guidance on AI in broking and algorithmic trading. Anti-discrimination obligations apply to all AI used in lending, insurance underwriting, and credit scoring. The penalty for non-compliance is not a fine — it is a directed remediation order that halts the AI system entirely.

RBI Model Risk Circular SEBI AI Guidelines Anti-Discrimination in Lending EU AI Act (if EU operations) ISO/IEC 42001
🩺

Healthcare & Life Sciences

High Urgency — FDA Guidance Active

Clinical AI — diagnostic models, treatment recommendation systems, patient risk scoring — must meet regulatory requirements that combine medical device law with AI-specific obligations. The FDA has issued binding guidance on AI/ML-based Software as a Medical Device (SaMD), requiring predetermined change control plans, real-world performance monitoring, and transparency documentation. Under the EU AI Act, clinical AI systems are classified as high-risk and must undergo conformity assessment before deployment. HIPAA obligations now extend to AI systems that process protected health information.

FDA AI/ML SaMD Guidance EU AI Act High-Risk HIPAA AI Obligations Clinical Validation Requirements NIST AI RMF
💻

Tech & SaaS

High Urgency — EU Market Access at Risk

If you sell a product into the EU — regardless of where it is built or hosted — the EU AI Act applies to you. AI features embedded in SaaS products, HR software, credit scoring tools, or recruitment platforms may meet the threshold for high-risk classification. Non-compliant AI products will not pass the conformity assessment required for EU market access. This is not a future risk — it is an active constraint on your 2025 sales motion into European markets. Compliance is now a product requirement, not a legal function afterthought.

EU AI Act (extraterritorial) GDPR AI Interactions ISO/IEC 42001 NIST AI RMF (US Federal) Enterprise Procurement Requirements
🏛️

Government & Defence

High Urgency — Procurement Requirement

AI procurement guidelines are now standard across UK, US, and Australian government frameworks. Any technology supplier to government that includes AI components must demonstrate compliance with applicable national AI guidelines and, for EU-connected procurement, the EU AI Act. The US federal government now references NIST AI RMF in procurement language. The UK’s AI Assurance framework is moving from voluntary to contractually required. Suppliers who cannot demonstrate AI compliance posture are being excluded from bids — not at award stage, but at qualification stage.

NIST AI RMF (US Federal) UK AI Assurance Framework Australian AI Ethics Principles EU AI Act (Government AI) ISO/IEC 42001
