What Failure Looks Like

These aren't hypotheticals. They happen to shipped AI products every week.

Every one of these failure modes is detectable before launch — if you run a structured red team. Most teams don't, until the incident is already in the news cycle.

🏦
Hallucination at Scale

Wrong tax guidance to 50,000 customers

"A bank's AI financial advisor, deployed to its retail customer base, confidently provides incorrect capital gains tax guidance for a specific instrument class — a variant it hadn't been evaluated on. 50,000 customers receive the output before the error surfaces."

Hallucinations don't fail silently. In regulated domains — financial advice, healthcare, legal — a hallucinated confident output is a compliance breach, not a UX issue. No amount of disclaimers reassigns the liability once customers act on it. The AI passed every scripted test. It hadn't been stress-tested on adversarial edge cases, rare instrument types, or jurisdictional variants.

🔓
Prompt Injection & Jailbreak

Proprietary system prompt extracted in 3 turns

"A competitor sends a series of seemingly innocuous queries to a SaaS vendor's AI assistant. Within three conversation turns, using nested instruction injection, they extract the full system prompt — including proprietary business logic, pricing rules, and internal product roadmap references."

System prompt leakage is one of the most underestimated attack surfaces in deployed LLM products. The majority of AI SaaS vendors embed proprietary logic, persona rules, and operational constraints in system prompts that are never meant to be user-visible. Standard penetration testing doesn't cover this. Only an LLM-specific red team does.

⚖️
Bias in Automated Decisions

AI hiring tool systematically disadvantages regional candidates

"An enterprise deploys an AI-assisted CV screening tool. Over six months, applicants from two specific regions of the country are declined at 3.4x the rate of equivalent applicants from other regions. The model had been trained on historical hiring data — and replicated historical hiring bias at scale and speed."

Bias in AI isn't always visible in aggregate accuracy metrics. It hides in conditional distributions — who the model gets wrong, and whether those errors are systematically correlated with protected characteristics. A standard eval suite won't surface this. A structured bias audit that slices by demographic subgroup, geography, and language variant will. The difference is whether your AI fails everyone equally or fails specific groups at scale.

🔀
Output Inconsistency

Same question, different answers — audit is impossible

"A pharmaceutical company deploys an AI to assist with regulatory documentation queries. An external audit reveals that the same compliance question, phrased in three different ways, produces three materially different answers across sessions — including one that contradicts the others. The audit trail is unauditable."

Output consistency is not the same as output accuracy. A system that is consistently accurate is deployable. A system that is inconsistently accurate — where the answer depends on phrasing, context, session order, or time of day — cannot be used in any process that requires documented, repeatable decisions. Consistency testing requires systematic variation of input phrasing, temperature probing, and cross-session comparison. It is not part of standard QA.

What Qapitol Tests

Eight attack categories. Forty-plus vectors. One structured red team report.

Every engagement runs all eight categories against your specific LLM use case, not a generic model. The attack surface for a customer support bot is different from a financial advisor or a code generation tool — and Qapitol adapts accordingly.

💉

Prompt Injection

Direct and indirect instruction injection attacks — including context-switching, role override, and nested instruction patterns that bypass system-level constraints.

🔐

Jailbreaking

Systematic attempts to bypass safety filters, persona constraints, and content policies — including multi-turn manipulation, encoded inputs, and role-playing escalation chains.

🌀

Hallucination Stress Testing

Edge case inputs, adversarial knowledge probes, and out-of-distribution queries designed to surface false confidence — especially in factual, regulatory, and domain-specific contexts.

⚖️

Bias & Fairness Audits

Demographic subgroup performance analysis, protected characteristic testing, geographic and linguistic variation testing — to surface systematic disparate impacts in AI decisions.

🔍

Data Leakage

Training data extraction probes, membership inference attacks, system prompt extraction, and PII leakage testing — including indirect leakage through context window manipulation.

🎭

Adversarial Inputs

Typographic attacks, homoglyph substitution, token boundary manipulation, and adversarial paraphrasing — inputs that appear normal to humans but systematically confuse model behaviour.

📋

Instruction Following Failures

Constraint adherence testing — does the model reliably follow its operational boundaries? Length limits, format requirements, topic restrictions, refusal triggers, and output guardrail bypass.

🔄

Output Consistency

Cross-session and cross-phrasing consistency analysis — does the model give materially different answers to semantically equivalent questions? Critical for auditable, regulated AI deployments.

The Assessment Process

From threat model to remediation playbook in 48 hours.

A Qapitol red team engagement is not a generic checklist. It maps to your specific LLM use case, attack surface, and regulatory context — then delivers a severity-classified findings report with specific, actionable remediations.

1

Threat Model Definition

We map the attack surface specific to your LLM deployment — the use case, user personas, input channels, output consumption, data access, and regulatory context. A customer support bot and a clinical decision support tool have completely different threat models. This step ensures we test what matters for you.

2

Red Team Execution

Specialist AI red teamers execute 40+ attack vectors against your model using Qapitol's QAVE platform for automated eval pipeline execution, combined with expert-led adversarial probing. Both automated scale and human creativity — because models that pass automated tests fail human attackers.

3

Risk Classification

Every finding is classified by severity (Critical / High / Medium / Low), likelihood of exploitation in your deployment context, and regulatory implication — mapped to relevant frameworks including NIST AI RMF, EU AI Act, OWASP LLM Top 10, and sector-specific requirements such as SR 11-7 and DORA.

4

Remediation Playbook

Not just a findings list — a playbook. Specific mitigations per finding: prompt hardening patterns, guardrail implementation, fine-tuning recommendations, retrieval layer changes, and ongoing monitoring setup. Every Critical and High finding includes a 30-day remediation timeline and a re-test slot.

QAVE AI Eval Engine

AI Simulation & Evaluation Platform

QAVE is Qapitol's AI simulation and evaluation engine — the platform that automates the execution of LLM evaluation pipelines at scale. In a red team engagement, QAVE handles the high-volume, systematic attack execution: running hundreds of adversarial prompt variants, recording responses, scoring outputs against defined safety and quality criteria, and surfacing statistical patterns that indicate systemic failure modes.

  • Automated execution of 40+ attack vector libraries across your deployed model
  • Adversarial prompt variant generation and response logging at scale
  • Statistical failure pattern detection — not just individual failure instances
  • Continuous regression evaluation to verify remediations held post-fix
  • Integrates with OpenAI, Anthropic, Azure OpenAI, Bedrock, and self-hosted models

CHEQ Compliance Platform

AI Governance & Compliance Reporting

CHEQ is Qapitol's AI governance and compliance platform — the system that turns red team findings into structured, regulator-ready documentation. CHEQ maps every finding to the relevant regulatory framework, generates the audit trail, and produces the evidence package needed for EU AI Act conformity assessments, NIST AI RMF implementations, and enterprise risk committee reporting.

  • Regulatory framework mapping: EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF, OWASP LLM Top 10, SR 11-7, DORA
  • Risk register generation with severity classification and ownership assignment
  • Evidence package production for conformity assessments and board-level reporting
  • Remediation tracking — links findings to mitigations and records re-test outcomes
  • Continuous monitoring dashboards for post-launch AI safety posture
