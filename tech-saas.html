WHERE PRODUCT AI GOES WRONG

AI Features Ship Fast — The Problems Arrive Faster

Every AI-powered feature in your SaaS product is a potential liability — hallucinated outputs, biased recommendations, and agentic actions that can't be audited. These are the failures your current QA process isn't catching.

🤖
['PRODUCT RISK']

LLM Hallucinations in Customer-Facing Features

Copilots and AI assistants that confidently generate incorrect information erode user trust, generate support tickets, and — for regulated-industry customers — create contractual liability.

📉
MODEL RISK

AI Model Degradation Between Releases

Upgrading the underlying model (GPT-4o → Claude 3.7 → Gemini 2.5) without systematic regression testing introduces silent quality regressions that your standard test suite doesn't catch.

⚖️
REGULATORY RISK

EU AI Act Classification Uncertainty

If your SaaS serves EU customers or enterprises in regulated sectors, your AI features may fall in the high-risk category — requiring conformity assessment, documentation, and a human oversight mechanism before go-live.

🔐
SECURITY RISK

Agentic Workflows Without Guardrails

AI agents that browse the web, call APIs, and modify data on behalf of users are a new attack surface. Without evaluation frameworks and permission boundaries, a single prompt injection can become a customer data incident.

COMPLIANCE CONTEXT

What Regulation Means for Your AI Product

Tech companies have historically operated under light AI regulation. That's ending fast — EU AI Act, DPDP, and enterprise customer contract requirements are creating new obligations for every SaaS product with AI features.

🇪🇺

EU AI Act

High-Risk Classification

SaaS AI features used in hiring, credit, education, or critical infrastructure automatically fall in the EU AI Act high-risk category — requiring conformity assessment and CE marking before sale into EU markets.

🔒

DPDP Act 2023

Indian Market

SaaS platforms with Indian users that process personal data through AI — recommendation engines, personalisation, behavioural analytics — must comply with DPDP consent and purpose limitation requirements.

🛡️

SOC 2 Type II

Enterprise Sales

Enterprise procurement teams now routinely require evidence of AI governance as part of SOC 2 audits. Documenting your AI evaluation and monitoring processes is a direct sales enabler.

📋

OWASP LLM Top-10

Security Standard

OWASP's LLM Top-10 has become the de facto security standard for LLM-powered applications — increasingly referenced in enterprise security questionnaires and penetration testing scopes.

🤝

Customer Contracts

Enterprise Liability

Enterprise SaaS contracts increasingly include AI liability clauses requiring documented model governance, accuracy SLAs, and incident response procedures for AI failures.
