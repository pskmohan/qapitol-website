The Hyperscaler Problem

OpenAI is not incentivized to tell you
your model is underperforming. We are.

80% of Fortune 500 companies now run AI on Azure AI Foundry. OpenAI, Microsoft, Google, and AWS have collectively become the enterprise AI deployment layer. That creates a structural problem most enterprises haven't named yet.

The auditor problem, applied to AI:

A company's external auditor cannot be employed by the company being audited — because the conflict of interest is structural. The same logic applies to AI. Your AI vendor is not the right entity to tell you whether your AI is performing correctly. They are financially incentivized to retain your deployment — not to surface degradation, drift, or compliance failures that might make you reconsider.

The US Enterprise AI Challenge

Speed is creating a governance gap.

US enterprises are shipping AI faster than any other region — and accumulating technical debt in evaluation, compliance, and quality infrastructure that will surface as very public failures.

Evaluation Gap
🔬

AI ships without rigorous eval

Most US teams run informal, ad-hoc evals before launch. There is no continuous evaluation in production. Hallucinations, drift, and safety failures accumulate invisibly until they become news.

Compliance Complexity
📋

Fragmented regulatory landscape

NIST AI RMF, SOC 2 AI controls, HIPAA for AI in healthcare, EU AI Act for US companies with EU exposure — no single team has the capacity to track and map all of these simultaneously.

Talent Constraint

AI engineering talent is expensive

A senior AI QA engineer in San Francisco costs $180K–$240K/year. Building a full evaluation capability requires 5–8 specialists. The math doesn’t work for most programmes.

Data Privacy

Production data is locked behind privacy

You cannot use real customer data to train and test AI systems at scale. Synthetic data that is production-faithful and privacy-safe is the only practical solution — and most teams don’t have it.
